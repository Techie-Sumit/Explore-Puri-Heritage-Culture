About Puri

Discover the rich history, vibrant culture, and spiritual significance of Puri - where ancient traditions blend seamlessly with modern coastal life.

History & Geography

Ancient Origins

Puri's history dates back to ancient times, with the magnificent Jagannath Temple built in the 12th century by King Anantavarman Chodaganga Deva. The city has been a major pilgrimage center for over 900 years, attracting devotees from across the Indian subcontinent.

Key Features

Strategic Location

Located on the Bay of Bengal coast, 60km from Bhubaneswar, the capital city of Odisha.

Diverse Population

Home to 2.5 lakh residents speaking Odia, Hindi, Bengali, and English in harmony.

Global Recognition

UNESCO World Heritage consideration for Jagannath Temple and Rath Yatra Festival.

Ancient History

Dating back to 12th century, with continuous religious and cultural practices for 900+ years.

Coastal Geography

Located on the shores of the Bay of Bengal, Puri enjoys a tropical climate with beautiful golden beaches stretching for miles. The nearby Chilika Lake, Asia's largest brackish water lagoon, adds to the region's natural beauty and biodiversity.

Cultural Significance

As one of the four sacred Char Dham pilgrimage sites, Puri holds immense spiritual importance in Hinduism. The annual Rath Yatra (Chariot Festival) is celebrated worldwide, making Puri a global symbol of devotion and cultural unity.

Historical Timeline

12th Century

King Anantavarman Chodaganga Deva constructs the magnificent Jagannath Temple, establishing Puri as a major pilgrimage center.

13th Century

Rath Yatra tradition begins, becoming one of India's most famous religious festivals, attracting millions of devotees annually.

16th Century

Saint Chaitanya Mahaprabhu visits Puri, spending his last 18 years here and establishing the city's Vaishnava traditions.

19th Century

British colonial period brings railway connectivity, making Puri more accessible to pilgrims from across India.

Modern Era

Puri transforms into a major tourist destination while preserving its ancient spiritual heritage and cultural traditions.

Notable Personalities

King Anantavarman Chodaganga

12th century ruler of Kalinga who built the Jagannath Temple and established Puri as a sacred pilgrimage site.

Sri Chaitanya Mahaprabhu

16th century saint and founder of Gaudiya Vaishnavism who spent his final years in Puri, deepening its spiritual significance.

Jagannath Das

16th century poet-saint who translated the Bhagavata Purana into Odia, making sacred texts accessible to common people.

Gajapati Maharaja

Traditional rulers of Puri who serve as the chief servitor of Lord Jagannath and maintain the temple's ancient rituals.

Quick Facts & Fun Facts

Char Dham Yatra

One of the four sacred pilgrimage sites in Hinduism along with Badrinath, Dwarka, and Rameswaram.

Rath Yatra Festival

World's largest religious procession with chariots as tall as 4-story buildings, celebrated globally.

Famous Khaja Sweet

Special layered sweet delicacy offered to Lord Jagannath, recognized with Geographical Indication tag.

Golden Beach

35km stretch of pristine coastline along the Bay of Bengal, perfect for sunrise views and beach activities.

Sacred Flag

Temple flag always flies in the opposite direction of wind - a phenomenon that amazes visitors and scientists.

Mahaprasad

Temple kitchen feeds 100,000+ people daily using traditional wood-fired cooking methods in earthen pots.
